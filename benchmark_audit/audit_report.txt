PASHTO IR BENCHMARK — DUPLICATION & CONSISTENCY AUDIT REPORT
=================================================================

This report distinguishes five separate dimensions. A finding in one dimension
does not imply anything about the others:
  - structural integrity   (IDs, required fields, target validity)
  - lexical similarity     (character/token overlap; a proxy signal only)
  - document duplication   (exact/near-duplicate document_text)
  - query duplication      (exact/near-duplicate query_text)
  - semantic relevance     (NOT assessed in this notebook — no LLM or human
                             judgment was used; nothing here is a relevance label)

1. Are duplicate documents mostly exact copies?
   115 duplicate-text groups found, covering 329 (32.9%) of 1000 documents. Of these groups, 7.8% have identical text AND identical metadata (Class A), and the remainder show some metadata mismatch — see duplicate_document_groups.csv for classification per group.

2. Do duplicate queries usually target duplicate documents?
   48.2% of duplicate query-text groups target either the same document or documents that are themselves exact/near-duplicates of each other.

3. How many duplicate queries target genuinely different documents?
   117 duplicate query-text groups (out of 226) target documents classified as moderately similar or clearly different from each other — see duplicate_query_groups.csv, target_relationship='different_documents'.

4. How many target documents have exact duplicates?
   329 queries (32.9%) have a target document with at least one exact-text duplicate elsewhere in the corpus — see duplicate_target_impact.csv.

5 & 6. Low-overlap queries: problematic vs. plausible hard queries.
   50 queries were flagged at the 5th-percentile TF-IDF cosine cutoff. As a rough cross-reference, 20 of these carry a 'hard' difficulty label and 30 do not. Difficulty-label agreement is only a weak proxy — each case in low_overlap_queries_with_full_text.csv needs a human read to distinguish a genuinely hard/indirect query from a possible query-target mismatch.

7. What proportion of the benchmark is affected by each issue?
   Document duplication : 32.9% of documents
   Query duplication     : 58.4% of queries (exact text)
   Near-duplicate queries: 60.3% of queries involved in a cosine>=0.90 pair
   Low lexical overlap    : 5.0% of queries (flagged tail)
   Any of the above       : 61.7% of queries

8. Which issues require manual review?
   - 117 duplicate query groups classified HIGH risk (identical query wording pointing at genuinely different documents)
   - 0 duplicate document groups classified HIGH risk (identical text with conflicting category/metadata, or other unusual patterns)
   - 50 low-lexical-overlap queries (Part 5/6)
   All three sets are exported to CSV for line-by-line human review; none were altered.

9. Which issues could bias retrieval metrics?
   - 329 queries with an exact-duplicate target document risk false
     negatives under strict single-ID retrieval evaluation (a correct retrieval of the
     duplicate would be scored as wrong).
   - 117 duplicate query groups pointing at genuinely different
     documents risk inconsistent gold-label behavior if the same query text is ever reused
     in train/eval splits without care.

10. Does the benchmark appear ready to freeze, or does it need another revision cycle?
    This notebook does not make that decision — it is evidence only. Based on the figures
    above, the reviewer should weigh: the scale of exact document duplication (32.9% of documents), the 117 duplicate-query
    groups pointing at different documents, and the manual-review outcome for the flagged
    low-overlap queries, before deciding whether to freeze, deduplicate, or regenerate
    the affected subset.
